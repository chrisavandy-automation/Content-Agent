# Hooks
Read by: any skill that writes hooks (posts, video titles and openers). Read `foundation/pillars.md` first.
Source: `sources/art-of-the-hook-lesson.md` (Jay Yang). Carried-over corrections: LinkedIn-Agent `Writing Coach/lessons.md` + `changelog.md`.

## Principle
The hook is the first 1-3 lines (on YouTube: the title, thumbnail and first seconds). Its only job is to stop the scroll. It is also the post's core idea: a better hook means a better idea, so hooks are written BEFORE the body.
Specific enough to grab the right people, broad enough to grab as many of them as possible.

## Anatomy: callout + promise
- **Callout:** who it's for (the cocktail-party effect: you hear your name through the noise).
- **Promise:** what they get for their time. Make them aware of it in the hook; pay it off near the end.

## The 10 hook types (tag ids used everywhere)
| Tag | Type | Shape (fill with my real details, never invent them) |
|---|---|---|
| `announcement` | Announcement + context | After [time/effort], I'm [launching/starting] [thing]. |
| `authority-how` | Authoritative sentence + how | I [result with a number]. Here's the [N]-step process: |
| `authority-lessons` | Authoritative sentence + lessons | I [result]. After [time], here's what I learned about [topic]: |
| `stance` | Declarative stance | The easiest way to [outcome] in [year] is [my method]. |
| `hot-take` | Hot take | If you're not [doing X], [consequence]. |
| `moment` | Moment in time | In [month year], I [moment]. |
| `question` | Thought-provoking question | Would you rather [A] or [B]? |
| `vulnerable` | Vulnerable statement | For the past [time], I've [struggled with X]. |
| `insight` | Unique insight | After [testing/analysing N things], I found [surprising finding]. |
| `icp-callout` | ICP callout | If you're a [who] who [struggle]: |
| `plain` | Plain (my addition) | A direct statement of what changed, in my everyday words. No contrast, twist or punchline. |

## Best practices (check every hook)
- [ ] Callout: is it clear who it's for?
- [ ] Promise: is it clear what they get?
- [ ] Curiosity gap: is something missing that they need the post to find out (an unexpected twist)?
- [ ] Specific: real numbers, times, tools ("after 118 enquiries" beats "after lots of testing").
- [ ] Credibility: a number, a borrowed name (credited), or first person (I / my).
- [ ] Recent language where true: "I just…", "I recently…".
- [ ] Speaks to the avatar: "If you've been struggling with…".

## My rules (from corrections on the LinkedIn-Agent, keep them)
- One of every set must be a `plain` hook. I often prefer it; never score it down for being plain. (20 Oct)
- Don't force a number into every hook; one strong proof number, or none. (7, 9 and 17 Oct)
- Take my short, human version over an engineered one. (9 Oct)

## How skills write hooks (the battle test)
1. Write the post's core idea and the reader's promise in one sentence each. If either is unclear, fix the idea first.
2. Write **5 hooks: 4 proven + 1 wildcard** (`system/wildcard.md`). One of the 4 proven is `plain`.
   - PROVEN: closest matches from `examples/hooks.md` and `examples/index.md` (same pillar + story type, best results first), plus the 10 types above. Name the example each one borrows from.
   - WILDCARD: a hook type or shape I haven't used. Say which reference it breaks from.
3. Check each against the best practices; lead with what's weak.
4. Pick a winner with a one-line why. I can overrule or mix.
5. Save the winner to `examples/hooks.md` (result filled in after posting).
