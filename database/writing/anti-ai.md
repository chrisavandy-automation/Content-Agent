# Anti-AI checklist (every draft, every hook set)
Read by: every skill that writes words I will publish. Run `python tools/slop_check.py <file>` on every draft before showing it to me. Source: `sources/ai-assist-and-viral-formats-lesson.md` (Jay Yang M3 L4), carried over from LinkedIn-Agent.
Merged from: Jay Yang Module 3 Lesson 4; Alex Vacca's post #4 (LinkedIn-Agent); and the rules in github.com/jalaalrd/anti-ai-slop-writing (no licence, so rules are summarised here, not copied. Read the source for the full word lists).
Where they conflict, LinkedIn style wins (marked ⚖).

## Pass/fail gate (any fail = fix before posting)
1. First person. It's my story, my client, my build.
2. No em dashes. (Skill allows 1 per 500 words; Jay says none. Use none.)
3. No "not just X, but Y" / "It's not about X, it's about Y".
4. No banned patterns: "Most people think X. The reality is Y." / "It's X. It's Y. It's Z." / "Everyone does X. Nobody does Y."
5. No 3 sentences of the same length in a row. ⚖ Short lines with line breaks are fine on LinkedIn; just vary them.
6. No invented stats, quotes or anecdotes. A made-up number stays a [bracket] or is framed as "imagine…/if…". (See lesson: 90% idea.)
7. Nothing any AI could have written for anyone: at least one specific (a real number, name, tool, time, or moment).
8. Read out loud: does it sound like me talking?

## The Do's (M3 L4). ALWAYS performed on every hook set and draft
1. Do write in first person (I / my / me, ideally in the hook).
2. Do provide unique, personal value (only I could have written it: my moment, my number, my client, my build).
3. Do vary sentence length (no 3 in a row of the same length).
4. Do use line breaks, colons and ellipses to break up long sections (max 2 ellipses; no block over ~45 words).
5. Do read the post out loud to see how it sounds (my job before posting; the coach reminds me every time).
Checks 1, 3 and 4 run automatically in `tools/slop_check.py`; 2 and 5 are printed as manual reminders.

## Word and phrase ban (posts only)
- Vocabulary, e.g.: delve, tapestry, landscape (figurative), testament, vibrant, pivotal, crucial, intricate, meticulous, bolster, garner, underscore, foster, leverage (verb), utilize, facilitate, paramount, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, game-changer, transformative, revolutionise, seamless, robust, unprecedented, remarkable, synergy, **pain points**, moving forward, circle back.
- Phrases, e.g.: "In today's…", "It's worth noting", "Let's dive in", "At its core", "When it comes to", "Here's the thing", "At the end of the day", "The bottom line is", "Unlock the power of", "Elevate / Streamline / Supercharge your…", "Bridge the gap", "Move the needle", "Take it to the next level", "In conclusion".
- Openers: Certainly, Absolutely, Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally, Interestingly, Notably, Importantly, Indeed, "Great question".

## Style rules
- Pick a side. One sentence max for the counterpoint. (Hot takes need a clear stance anyway: Jay M3 L2.)
- Active voice. Contractions. Simple words.
- Use specifics: real numbers, named tools (GHL, Claude, Meta), real moments ("last Tuesday at 11pm").
- Include friction or mess. Real builds break.
- ⚖ Ellipses: max 2 per post, never as a lazy transition. Colons to set up a payoff.
- ⚖ Threes only when there really are three items.
- Not every paragraph needs a wrap-up line. Sometimes just stop.
- Formatting: no markdown headers or random bold; no emoji bullets; 0 to 2 hashtags; no "🧵".

## Self-check before showing me any draft
Banned words? Same-length run? Default threes? Hedging? Em dash? Passive? Every paragraph wrapped up? Anything fabricated? Could anyone have written it? Sounds like ChatGPT? Fix, then show.
