# How to Pick Your Niche & Content Pillars (LinkedIn course lesson)
Fed in 2026-10-25. Raw source: don't edit. Distilled into `database/foundation/pillars.md`.

Charlie Munger once quipped, "All I want to know is where I will die so I'll never go there."
The mental model he was talking about is called: Inversion.
Rather than trying to be brilliant and get absolutely everything perfect, it's much easier to focus on avoiding the simple mistakes that can "kill" you.
The #1 reason why people give up building their personal brand on LinkedIn is because they don't know what to write about.

## What Is A Brand?
Fundamentally, a brand is the result of branding. And branding is the pairing of two (ideally relevant) things together. Your "brand" is what people associate you with.
Example: Nike → victory, greatness, athletes, winning, because they've paired themselves with winning athletes (Michael Jordan, Serena Williams, Tiger Woods) over and over again.
Brand = the result. Branding = the action.
Reverse engineer it: instead of letting your customers make associations FOR you, you make the associations for THEM.
1. What outcome do you want?
2. What do you need to be associated for?
3. What do you need to write about to be associated for those things?
Example (fitness coach helping athletes jump higher): write about increasing my vertical from 12 in. to 35 in.; how I helped clients increase theirs; how lost I felt when my basketball career ended and how I found a new calling.

## Influence
Influence = your ability to change other people's behaviours. It's a continuum, not a binary.
- Negative: I tell you to do something, you do the opposite.
- Positive: I tell you to do something, you do it.
- Neutral: I tell you to do something, you ignore it.

## The PTL Framework
### Power
Power = your ability to affect change in the world: money, status, network, expertise, leverage. We listen to powerful people because they have what we want. Power is situational (the bartender has power at the bar, not outside it).
To increase power, SHOW that you have what others want.
Power exercise: What do your customers want, internally and externally?
- Internally: love, meaning, joy, belonging.
- Externally: money? status? big house? cool cars? hot spouse?
Show power 2 ways: verbally ("Last year my portfolio did $250m in rev") and visually (a picture of your big building).

### Trust
Trust = the belief that following your direction will produce a good result.
Belief without evidence = faith. Belief WITH evidence = trust.
Increase trust by giving people directions to follow that get positive results (parents: "eat your veggies"; Martha Stewart's recipes). Each followed direction that works makes the next one more likely to be followed.
Trust exercise: What step-by-step directions can you give your customers to get positive results? (e.g. use these 50 hooks; read this book on storytelling; use this tool to automate posting.)

### Likeness
Likeness = how similar you are to the person you're trying to influence. In psychology, the in-group effect: people show favouritism, trust and empathy toward their in-group, and skepticism toward people outside it.
- External: physical attributes, what you wear, how you talk.
- Internal: beliefs, values, worldview.
Likeness exercise: What are your customers' internal and external attributes? (e.g. ex-athletes who wear athletic clothing, mid 30s-40s, work a 9-5, want to get back in shape, feel athletic, feel powerful, take agency over their life.)

Summary: to increase positive influence, demonstrate PTL in your content.

## The ARR Framework (3 content types that demonstrate PTL)
1. Authority (demonstrate competence): educational content, personal lessons (what you wish you knew), client stories.
2. Reach (reach as many people as possible): trend jacking, humour posts, viral quotes. Going viral matters: if people don't know about you, they can't be influenced by you.
3. Resonance (build connection): personal stories (I once thought X, now I think Y), core values, contrarian takes.

## Niche by transformation, not topic
If you help people go from A to Z, you can talk about any subtopic that helps them get there. (Vertical jump → confidence, purpose, being a dad, habits. Baking for stay-at-home mums → meal planning, budgeting, family time. Six-figure business → productivity, relationships, fitness, leadership, hiring, resilience, finance.)
Your niche isn't the subject you discuss. Your niche is the person you help become someone else.
What's my niche? → What do I have a unique insight on, because of my experience and curiosities, that others need to hear about?

## Summary checklist (before you publish)
- Who am I trying to help?
- What transformation am I helping them achieve? (A → Z)
- Does this post increase my Power, Trust or Likeness?
- Is this an Authority, Reach or Resonance post?
- Will this help people associate me with the outcome I want to be known for?
- If someone binge-read my last 20 posts, would they know exactly what I help people achieve?
People don't follow you because you talk about a topic. They follow you because, over time, they believe you're the person who can help them become who they want to be.
