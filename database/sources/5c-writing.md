# The 5Cs of Writing (+ Hooks, Bodies, CTAs)
Fed in 2026-10-25 (Chris's copy of Jay Yang's 5Cs, with employer-specific references removed). Raw source: don't edit. Distilled into `database/writing/gate.md` and `writing/structures.md`.


**The mindset.** This is real-world writing, the opposite of school writing. In school a teacher is paid to read you, and long words make you look smart. Online, **nobody owes you their attention**. It's not about how smart the writer sounds; it's about how smart the reader *feels*.

> **Write for someone busy, distracted, with below a fifth-grade reading level.**

**The goal is clarity, not art.** The best copy is like a shop window: you don't look at the glass, you look at what's behind it. Copy that draws attention to itself is in the way of the offer.

---

## Part 1: The 5Cs

### C1. Clear

They can't be persuaded by what they don't understand.

- **Method:** don't try to write more clearly; **remove anything that's unclear**. Edit from the end back to the front.
- **The firefighter test.** Unclear: "Hey guys, um, there's a situation downstairs that we may need to think about…" Clear: "There's a fire downstairs. Open the window and jump."
- **Tool:** Hemingway App grades the reading level and highlights sentences to cut or rewrite.

### C2. Concise

No wasted words.

- **Pretend every word costs $10.**
- **The question before publishing:** *What can I cut without losing quality or understanding?*
- **Practice:** short-form writing with a hard limit (for example, 10+ short posts a day for 6 months). A character limit forces concision.

### C3. Concrete

Make it **visualisable**. The reader should be able to picture it.

- ❌ "We help business owners grow."
- ✅ "We took a roofing company from 14 leads a month to 70."
- **For big, abstract ideas, zoom in.** "I love you" is hard to picture. A specific, sensory moment isn't. Use this especially when describing emotions.

### C4. Conversational

Write how your reader talks.

- **Test:** read it out loud. If no one would actually say it, rewrite it.
- **Match the in-group's language.** 20-year-old students and 50-year-old lawyers need different words. For a young audience, "please don't hesitate to reach out" becomes "just text me."
- This is why knowing your audience matters. You can't sound like them if you don't know who they are.
- Avoid corporate, robotic school habits on social and in ads.

### C5. Cadence

Writing should read like music. A monotone drone bores readers, and bored readers don't buy.

> *The classic "write like music" passage:* This sentence has five words. Here are five more words. Five-word sentences are fine. But several together become monotonous. Listen to what is happening. The writing is getting boring. The sound of it drones. It's like a stuck record. The ear demands some variety. Now listen. I vary the sentence length, and I create music. Music. The writing sings. It has a pleasant rhythm, a lilt, a harmony. I use short sentences. And I use sentences of medium length. And sometimes, when I am certain the reader is rested, I will engage him with a sentence of considerable length, a sentence that burns with energy and builds with all the impetus of a crescendo, the roll of the drums, the crash of the cymbals, sounds that say listen to this, it is important.

**Two tests:**
1. **Read it out loud.** Anywhere you stumble, rewrite.
2. **The squint test.** Squint at the shape of the text. Line lengths should vary, not run as one flat block.

## Part 2: Hooks


> People read about five times as many headlines as body copy.

**The hook drives about 90% of performance.**

### The hook formula

```
              Benefit × Relevance × Credibility
Hook power =  ─────────────────────────────────
                     Perceived effort
```

| Factor | Question | Want it… |
|---|---|---|
| **Benefit** | Why should they care? What do they get? | High |
| **Relevance** | How many people does this apply to? | High (for the target avatar) |
| **Credibility** | Why listen to *you*? | High |
| **Perceived effort** | How hard is it to consume *and* implement? | **Low** |

**Example:** "I was employee #30 at a tech giant. Then I got fired. … The lessons I learned from its founder helped me build a $100M/year company. 10 lessons I learned working directly under him."
- Credibility: employee #30
- Benefit: 10 lessons
- Relevance: applies to almost anyone
- Effort: low. You get the founder's lessons without needing access to him.

### Hook archetypes (examples, not templates to copy blindly)

- **Identity:** call out who it's for. "Business owners…", "Marketers…"
- **Outcome / qualifier:** "If you're ambitious but inconsistent, watch this." "If you're doing £1–3M a year, keep watching."
- **Promise:** "We'll deliver X in Y, or you don't have to Z."
- **Buyer-quote flip:** turn a verbatim buyer statement into a hook. This is the strongest source.

> Warning: templates without the thinking behind them just get repeated. Know *why* a hook works (the formula above) before you reach for a pattern.

## Part 3: Bodies

Two universal structures:

### What / Why / How (educational)
1. **What.** The point you're making
2. **Why.** Why they should care
3. **How.** How to do it

*Example:* "Drink more water (what). It helps you think clearly, speak articulately, makes your skin glow and boosts energy (why). Drink an 8oz glass before every meal (how)."

### But / Therefore (story)
Avoid "and then… and then…", which is linear with no tension. Alternate **but** and **therefore** to build tension and surprise.

*Example:* "I went to the grocery store, **but** I forgot my keys. **Therefore** I went home, **but** someone was in my kitchen…"

## Part 4: Calls to action

The point is a business result, so the CTA matters. Use **Who / What / When / How**:

| Element | Example |
|---|---|
| **Who** you're looking for | "If you're a copywriter starting from zero who wants your first high-paying client in 90 days…" |
| **What** they should do | "…download my free workbook…" |
| **When** | "…fill it out in 20 minutes…" |
| **How** | "…click the link below." |

> **One CTA → one avatar → one offer.**

## Part 5: Editing checklist (run on every draft)

| Check | Pass condition |
|---|---|
| **Clear** | Passes the firefighter test; reading level around grade 5 |
| **Concise** | Nothing can be cut without losing meaning ($10/word) |
| **Concrete** | At least one specific number, name or picture; no vague "grow / help / transform" |
| **Conversational** | Sounds natural read aloud; uses the avatar's own words |
| **Cadence** | Sentence lengths vary; passes the squint test |
| **Hook** | Benefit, relevance and credibility are clear in the first line; perceived effort is low |
| **Body** | Follows What/Why/How or But/Therefore; no linear "and then" |
| **CTA** | Who/What/When/How present; exactly one CTA, one avatar, one offer |
