## Using AI to assist
While AI won't replace you as a writer, it's great to help you come up with ideas, iterate quicker, and clean up your work.
1. Brain dump with voice dictation. Before I start writing, I talk through my ideas out loud to AI using WisprFlow (or the built-in speech-to-text tool). Don't worry about how organised your thoughts are; you're just giving AI context. Speaking is quicker than writing, and it naturally comes out in your own voice, which helps AI give you a better output.
2. Use AI to brainstorm. Feed your raw thoughts into Stanley, Claude, or whatever tool you prefer, and use it to: clean up your brain dump into a clear story or post structure; generate post ideas from the raw material; feed your ideas into high-performing templates (the viral formats or other references from your niche); get direction on which angle might resonate most with your audience.
3. Iterate. Get a lot more specific; iterate on different versions to find the best one. Ask AI to: brainstorm 5 hooks for the same post; try different post structures for the same idea (a list? a story? a hot take?); edit and tighten your draft to fit your tone of voice.
Don't be afraid to use your judgement. Nobody wants to see another AI slop post, so use this step to intentionally create a post that feels right for your audience.

## The Anti-AI Checklist
AI can very quickly start sounding generic. LinkedIn even introduced a feature to report a post as AI slop.
Do: write in first person; provide unique, personal value; vary your sentence length; use line breaks, colons and ellipses to break up long sections; read the post out loud.
Don't: use em-dashes; use the "not just X, but Y" format; write with complex vocabulary (keep it simple); repeat the same sentence length back to back to back.
A Claude skill that prevents most AI tendencies: github.com/jalaalrd/anti-ai-slop-writing.

## The Three Viral Formats (from analysing thousands of posts)
1. Value-based virality: insanely useful ("I need to save this", "send this to my team").
   Virality = Benefit x Relevance x Credibility / Perceived Effort
   - Benefit: focus on the outcome, appeal to emotion, reduce uncertainty.
   - Relevance: speak to your reader, hijack trends, use their lingo.
   - Credibility: use numbers, borrow authority, share personal experiences.
   - Lowered perceived effort: make it simple, save them time, use social proof.
   (Jay's deep-dive deck: captured in `sources/viral-hook-science-deck.md`.)
2. Shock-based virality: the reader feels something so intense they have to engage. Works best for controversial posts, but any genuine surprise works.
   Virality = Emotional Charge x Relevance x Novelty
   - Emotional charge: inspired, annoyed, upset, happy, heard, fired up; intensity matters more than which emotion.
   - Relevance: speak to your reader, hijack trends, use their lingo.
   - Novelty: is this a purple cow?
   - Examples: Timothy's Spotify Wrapped intern post (linkedin.com/posts/timothy-t-y_i-just-found-out-that-spotify-wrapped-was-share-7270718980053417984-qeHJ); Roy Lee kicked out of Columbia (roy-lee-goat_i-just-got-kicked-out-of-columbia-for-taking-share-7310834404073828352-U1v8).
3. Story-based virality: the story is so compelling people can't stop reading.
   Virality = Transformation Gap x Relevance x Novelty
   - Transformation gap: the distance between before and after; the wider, the better. Open with a teaser of the transformation and the BEFORE state; pull the reader into the lowest point first. Tangible numbers for scale ("10+ job offers", "0 to 50K followers"). A before/after image.
   - Relevance, Novelty: as above.
   - Examples: Michael (myan_before-i-turned-down-a-230000-job-offer-activity-7341547406699712516-0-v8); Kevin (kevinshim1_at-19-i-applied-to-40-internships-and-got-activity-7472702036942274560-VUr2).

## Summary checklist (before publishing)
- Is there something unique about this post (a fresh take, personal experience, a perspective only I can share)?
- Does it make the reader feel a specific emotion? If not really, edit it.
- Does it entertain, educate or inspire, and do at least one really well?
- Which viral format? Value: is the benefit obvious, relevant, credible, easy to act on? Shock: is the charge strong enough, the take actually surprising? Story: is the transformation gap wide enough?
- Would I save, share or comment on this if I saw it in my own feed?
