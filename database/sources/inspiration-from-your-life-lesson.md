"Everybody walks past a thousand story ideas every day. The good writers are the ones who see five or six of them. Most people don't see any." ― Orson Scott Card

There's a common saying in the content space that, in order to build a magnetic personal brand online, you have to do cool shit offline.
While I do believe that's true, I also believe it leads many people astray.
The truth is, the best content is not about the big, flashy moments. It's actually about when we zoom in on the small, seemingly mundane moments and bring them to life.
As I'm making this lesson, of the last 20 posts I've posted on LinkedIn, 11 of those posts are me sharing a small moment of my life:
- A conversation with an old friend
- Positive feedback on my upcoming book
- Getting featured in The Economic Times
- An update on my book launch
- Feeling nervous about being a YouTuber
- Changing my bio and banner
- Feeling excited about the future
- Getting followed back by a personal hero
- Another update on my book launch
(Links to each are on Jay's LinkedIn, linkedin.com/in/jayyanginspires.)

"That's a lot of little moments, Jay. How do you notice and capture them so consistently?"
Good question. I use a "Story Bank".
A story bank is an exercise I learned from the book Storyworthy.
At the end of each day, take 5 minutes to reflect on the one thing that was most story-worthy that day. Write down a few words, maybe a sentence or two, so that when you revisit it in the future, it triggers your memory and you can remember the story better.
I've been doing this since 2023. I like to use WisprFlow or some voice transcription tool to speak out loud what happened during the day.
Then, when I'm writing posts, I can revisit those days and get inspiration.

There are 6 main types of stories you can include in your LinkedIn posts:

1. Far past (when I was 15...)
- What's something you tried years ago that didn't work but taught you a lesson you still use?
- Think back to when you first started out. What's a small moment that shaped how you work today?
- What did you believe about success when you were younger that turned out to be wrong?

2. Recent past (last week XYZ happened)
- What happened in the last week or two that surprised you or made you rethink something?
- Think about a decision you made recently. What was going through your head, and how did you land on it?
- What's a small win from this past week that taught you something worth sharing?

3. Current (I woke up this morning...)
- What did your morning look like today, and what does it say about the life you're building?
- What are you working on right now that a younger you wouldn't believe?
- What's something you're doing today that's moving you closer to a bigger goal?

4. Future (In 2027, I have a goal of...)
- What's one goal you have for this year, and why does it actually matter to you?
- Where do you want to be 12 months from now, and what's the first step you're taking?
- If the next year goes exactly right, what does your life look like, and what did you have to do to get there?

5. Client stories (I had a client ask me...)
- What's a question people ask you over and over, and how do you answer it?
- Think of someone you recently helped. What was their problem, and how did you solve it?
- What's a common mistake you see people make in your world, and what do you tell them to do instead?

6. Hypothetical (Imagine you came to me and asked...)
- If you had to hit a big goal from scratch in 90 days, what would you do?
- Imagine someone asked you for advice on the thing you know best. What steps would you walk them through?
- If you had to start over tomorrow, what would you do differently knowing what you know now?

How to manufacture your own momentum online:
1. Tell people what you're going to do
2. Do it
3. Tell people you did it
4. Remind people you did it
5. Tell people what you're going to do next
6. 🔁

I'll give you an example:
Whenever I'm thinking of building a product (whether that's this Publish30 cohort, writing a book, or starting a YouTube channel), the first thing I do is ask people if they'd be interested in that. If they were, what would they like to see?
1. That validates the idea.
2. It gives me ideas on what to actually build.
3. Once I commit to doing it, I like to tease that I'm working on it and get people excited for it.

There are three parts to that:
1. Whisper: What you want to say is that you're working on something, but you don't tell them what it is.
2. Tease: Tell them that you're working on the thing, but don't reveal a lot.
3. Shout: Once you launch it, try to be everywhere, tell everyone about it, etc.

Once it's launched, what you want to do is start sharing wins (like the product winning, clients winning, or things going well).
A few months later, you can remind people of that amazing thing.
- "Wow, I can't believe I launched my book over a year ago. That's crazy."
- "I can't believe it's been six months since I started my YouTube channel."
- "Oh my gosh, I've written a newsletter every single week for the last 52 weeks."

Do you see how you can manufacture your own momentum in public?
The key idea: yes, focus on doing cool shit offline. But equally as important, focus on being aware of the cool things that are already happening in your life. Then be relentless about sharing them.
